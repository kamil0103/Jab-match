Got it — a **general resume guide**, not tailored to any specific person.

# What a Good Resume Should Have

A standard modern resume usually has:

1. **Contact Information**
2. **Professional Summary** (optional)
3. **Skills**
4. **Work Experience**
5. **Education**
6. **Projects** (if relevant)
7. **Certifications / Awards** (optional)

The order can change depending on your situation.

---

# 1. Contact Information

Include:

✅ Full name
✅ Phone number
✅ Professional email
✅ LinkedIn (if applicable)
✅ Portfolio/GitHub (for tech/design roles)

Avoid:

❌ Full home address
❌ Photo (in most countries, especially US)
❌ Age/date of birth
❌ Personal information unrelated to work

---

# 2. Professional Summary (Optional)

Only use it if it adds value.

Length:
**2–4 lines maximum**

Good:

> Marketing professional with 5 years of experience managing digital campaigns and increasing customer engagement through data-driven strategies.

Bad:

> Hardworking individual seeking a challenging position where I can grow.

Everyone says that.

---

# 3. Skills Section

This is where many people make mistakes.

## How many skills should you list?

Usually:

**10–15 skills total**

A good range:

* Entry level: **8–12**
* Experienced: **10–20**
* Very experienced: **15–25**

Do not add 50 skills just to look impressive.

Recruiters assume:

* You barely know them
* You are keyword stuffing
* You cannot defend them in an interview

---

## Skill Categories

Instead of:

```
Skills:
Python, Java, SQL, Leadership, Communication, Excel, Git, Photoshop
```

Use categories:

```
Technical Skills:
Python, Java, SQL, Git

Software:
Excel, Photoshop, Figma

Languages:
Spanish, English
```

---

# Skill Rule

Only include something if you can answer:

> "How did you use this?"

Example:

Good:

> Microsoft Excel

because you can explain:
"I used pivot tables and formulas for reports."

Bad:

> Machine Learning

because you watched two tutorials.

---

# 4. Work Experience

This is usually the most important section.

Format:

## Job Title

Company | Dates

Bullet points:

* Action
* What you did
* Result

Example:

Weak:

> Answered phones.

Strong:

> Managed customer inquiries and resolved issues while maintaining high customer satisfaction.

---

# How Many Bullet Points?

Per job:

Most recent job:
**3–5 bullets**

Older jobs:
**2–3 bullets**

Do not write a whole paragraph.

---

# 5. Education

Include:

* Degree
* School
* Graduation date (or expected date)
* Relevant honors (optional)

Example:

```
Bachelor of Science in Computer Science
University Name
Expected Graduation: 2027
```

Do not include:

* Elementary school
* High school (usually after college)
* Every class you took

---

# 6. Projects

Important for:

* Students
* Career changers
* Developers
* Designers

Each project should have:

**Name**
**Tools used**

Then:

* What you created
* What problem it solved
* What technologies you used

Example:

```
Inventory Management System
Python, SQL

- Created a system to track inventory and generate reports.
- Designed database structure and automated data processing.
```

---

# 7. Certifications

Add only relevant ones.

Examples:

Good:

* AWS certification for cloud jobs
* Security+ for cybersecurity
* Google Analytics for marketing

Bad:

* Random certificates unrelated to the job

---

# Resume Length

Most people:

## 1 page

Good for:

* Students
* New graduates
* Under ~5 years experience

## 2 pages

Okay for:

* Experienced professionals
* Managers
* Specialists

Avoid:

❌ 3+ pages unless you are very senior

---

# Formatting Rules

Use:

✅ Clean font
Examples:

* Arial
* Calibri
* Helvetica
* Inter

Font size:

Name:
16–20

Body:
10–12

Margins:
0.5–1 inch

---

# Things To Avoid

## ❌ "References available upon request"

Waste of space.

---

## ❌ Skill ratings

Example:

Python ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Recruiters do not trust this.

---

## ❌ Personal statements

Example:

> My dream is to work for a company that changes the world...

Save that for interviews.

---

## ❌ Too many colors

Professional resumes are usually:

* Black text
* Simple layout
* Maybe one accent color

---

## ❌ Spelling mistakes

One typo can make you look careless.

---

# Resume Keywords (ATS)

Many companies use software called ATS (Applicant Tracking System).

If the job asks for:

> Project management, Excel, SQL, Python

Your resume should contain those exact terms if true.

Do not replace:

"Database management"

with:

"Organized information"

The system may not recognize it.

---

# Best Bullet Formula

Use:

**Action + Skill/Tool + Result**

Examples:

Weak:

> Made reports.

Strong:

> Created automated Excel reports that reduced manual data entry time.

---

# Quick Resume Checklist

Before submitting:

✅ One page (if possible)
✅ No spelling mistakes
✅ Skills match the job
✅ Uses action verbs
✅ Shows results, not just duties
✅ Easy to read in 10 seconds
✅ Saved as PDF
✅ File name looks professional:

Good:

```
John_Smith_Resume.pdf
```

Bad:

```
resume_final_final2_REAL.pdf
```

---

The biggest mistake people make is trying to put **everything they have ever done**. A resume is a filtered highlight reel, not a biography.
